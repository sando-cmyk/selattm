=== Slide 25: The TMP Form Set and a Good Diagram  ->  slide25.m4a  (151 words, about 65 s) ===
Module six: putting it on paper. The NZGTTM TMP has three parts. The Planning Form is for the risk reviewer and approver. It holds the risks, the controls you chose and why, the authorisations, and delay information. The General Form is for everyone, including site staff: the location and road details, programme, transport aspects affected, temporary speed limits, contingencies. And there's a TMD Form for each layout, for example attended and unattended, or installation and removal. Each TMD Form has its own times, specific risks, and the installation, site and removal methodology. A good diagram lets an STMS set the site out from the drawing alone. That means road names, a north point, a legend, every sign and device, the work area and zones, and the key dimensions. Label them in metres, and in seconds where it helps, so the set-out can be checked on site by timing a fast vehicle.

=== Slide 27: Activity: Capstone Lane Closure  ->  slide27.m4a  (123 words, about 53 s) ===
Time for the capstone. Here's the site. A rural two-lane road, permanent limit a hundred kilometres an hour, but a speed survey shows an operating speed of eighty-two through this section, and the layout will hold traffic to eighty. It's sealed. The left lane will be closed for a hundred and twenty metres to renew a culvert, with a trench eight hundred millimetres deep at the edge line. There's a two hundred and fifty metre radius right-hand curve three hundred metres before the site, and twelve percent of the traffic is heavy vehicles. Work through each step in order. Write your answer down, then tap to check. When you've finished, you'll have every dimension you need to draw the diagram in the banner.

=== Slide 28: Self-Check and Next Steps  ->  slide28.m4a  (156 words, about 67 s) ===
Before you submit any TMP, run this check. Have you stated the design speed, and shown how it's achieved? Are the sign visibility, spacing and warning distance right for that speed, with room for queues? Are the exclusion zones sized for the surface, the traffic and the hazard? Are the tapers, the gaps between them, the lane widths and the delineation right, and is every taper off a tight curve? Have you covered workers on foot and any mobile work? Could an STMS set the site out from your diagram alone? And where you've changed a table value, have you written down why?
