Activity: Complete the Planning Form

Same cabinet-repair site as before. For each risk, write the control, its hierarchy level and your reasoning (including options discounted) – as you would in the TMP Planning Form. Then tap to compare.

0 of 3 explored

Risk 1: Pedestrians, including school children, pushed into the live lane

Control: A temporary footpath past the cabinet, physically separated from traffic and from the work, plus scheduling the noisiest work away from school finishing time.

Hierarchy level: Isolate (separation), supported by administrative (timing).

Why: Closing the footpath was considered and discounted – it would send children across a 50 km/h arterial where drivers don’t expect them, a higher total risk.

Risk 2: Low sun hides signs and workers from westbound drivers

Control: Finish the work before the sun is low, and place advance signs where they stay visible against the glare.

Hierarchy level: Administrative (timing), supported by engineering (sign placement).

Why: Working in the low-sun window was discounted. A temporary speed limit for the whole street was considered, but speed is reduced only where needed, as long sites with unexplained limits get ignored.

Risk 3: Work van entering and leaving traffic

Control: Park the van inside the protected work area on the verge, so no lane is occupied, and enter and exit only when traffic allows.

Hierarchy level: Isolate, supported by administrative (entry and exit procedure).

Why: A lane closure to park the van was considered and discounted – it would block a lane on an arterial for no safety gain.

THE TEST

Could a reviewer, your STMS or a court follow your reasoning from what you wrote? If not, the planning process has failed.
