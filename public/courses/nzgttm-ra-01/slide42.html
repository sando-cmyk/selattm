Monitoring: Active and Inactive Hours

~1 min · narrated

The STMS’s primary duty is the safety of workers and the public. Drive, walk and cycle through the site regularly.

Each shift, check

  • Signs and cones correctly positioned, secure and visible
  • Manual and ITS operations consistent with other signs
  • Barriers still meet the manufacturer’s specification
  • TMAs and arrow boards working as intended

Inactive hours

  • Separate controls, planned in the TMP
  • No workers = a different risk profile
  • Devices must keep working as designed
DON’T OVER-CONTROL

Excessive TTM undermines the public’s trust – and TTM that people ignore doesn’t work.

Once the site is set up, the STMS monitors how well it's working. Drive, walk and cycle through the site regularly. Check the controls are working as expected and there aren't any unexpected risks. At the start of and during each shift, check that signs and cones are still correctly positioned, signs are secure and visible from the right sight distance, manual and ITS operations match the other signs, barriers still meet the manufacturer's specification, and TMAs and arrow boards still work as intended. Inactive hours need their own controls in the TMP, because with no workers on site the risk profile is different. And don't over-control. If the public stop trusting that the TTM is there for a reason, they stop paying attention to it, and it stops working.
