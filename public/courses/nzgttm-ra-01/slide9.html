Describe Every Risk in Three Parts

~1 min · narrated

Describe risks carefully – otherwise you could choose the wrong control and end up increasing the risk.

PartWhat it isExample
1. HazardThe source of the riskVegetation hides the advance warning sign on the approach
2. EventWhat could happen because of the hazardA driver is unaware of the queue ahead and doesn’t slow
3. ConsequenceThe impact of the event, and on whomRear-end collision injuring people in the queued vehicles
KEY IDEA

A good description tells you exactly what you need to control.

If you describe a risk badly, you can choose the wrong control and end up increasing the risk. So the NZGTTM asks you to describe every risk in three parts. First, the hazard: the source of the risk. Second, the event that could result from that hazard. Third, the consequence: the impact of that event, and who it falls on. Here's an example. Vegetation is hiding an advance warning sign. That's the hazard. A driver comes around the bend unaware there's a queue ahead. That's the event. The driver runs into the back of the queue, injuring people in the stopped vehicles. That's the consequence. A description like that tells you exactly what you need to fix.
