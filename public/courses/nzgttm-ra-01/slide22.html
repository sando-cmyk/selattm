Matching Planning Effort to Risk

~1 min · narrated

The level of planning should be consistent with the level of risk – more risk means more planning effort.

CommonIn-betweenSite-specific
Repeated tasks, e.g. maintenance. A pre-approved scheme and risk assessment may suit – still checked for fit. A pre-approved scheme plus an updated job safety analysis (JSA) and site-specific checks. A detailed risk assessment. May need a TIA, traffic modelling, road safety audit or temporary works.

Before reusing a pre-approved scheme, ask:

  • What are the differences in the environment and activity?
  • Do those differences affect the risk assessment?
  • Are there new or different stakeholders?
  • Are additional controls (such as a JSA) needed?
The effort you put into planning should be consistent with the level of risk: more risk means more planning. The NZGTTM shows this as a scale. At one end are common sites, often repeated maintenance tasks, where a pre-approved reusable scheme and risk assessment may be suitable. They still need a risk assessment, a TMP and diagrams, and a check that they fit. In the middle, a pre-approved scheme might be used with an updated job safety analysis. At the other end are site-specific sites, which need a detailed risk assessment, and possibly specialist inputs like a traffic impact assessment, traffic modelling, a road safety audit or temporary works design. Before reusing any scheme, ask: what's different about this activity and environment? Do the differences change the risk? Are there new stakeholders? And are extra controls needed? If a plan doesn't fit, don't use it.
