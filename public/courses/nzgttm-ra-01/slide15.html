Activity: Spot the Risks

~2 min (interactive) · narrated
THE SITE

A utility crew needs to repair a cabinet on the grass verge of a busy two-lane suburban arterial (50 km/h). The footpath runs right past the cabinet. There’s a bus stop 30 m further along, a primary school 200 m away, and work is planned for 1–4 pm on a school day. The road runs east–west.

Tap each hotspot. Try to describe the hazard, event and consequence yourself before you open it.

0 of 4 explored

Footpath closed

Hazard: No footpath or verge past the cabinet, at school finishing time.

Event: Pedestrians – including children – cross the arterial where drivers aren’t expecting them, or walk in the live lane.

Consequence: A pedestrian is struck by a vehicle – serious injury or death. Drivers swerving to avoid them also put others at risk.

Bus stop nearby

Hazard: A bus stop 30 m from the site, with passengers getting on and off.

Event: Passengers move unpredictably around the site; a stopped bus blocks sight lines to the TTM.

Consequence: Passengers struck by traffic, or a rear-end collision behind the bus. Public transport users and drivers are at risk.

Work van access

Hazard: Crew vehicles need to enter and leave the live traffic stream.

Event: A van pulls out or stops suddenly at an unsafe entry or exit point.

Consequence: Collision with general traffic – injury to the crew and road users.

Afternoon sun

Hazard: Low sun on an east–west road in the afternoon.

Event: Westbound drivers are dazzled and can’t see the signs or workers in time.

Consequence: A driver hits equipment or a worker – serious injury to the crew.

NOTICE

Closing the footpath protects pedestrians from the cabinet but creates a new risk. That’s lowest total risk – covered in Module 4.

Time to practise. Read the site description, then tap each hotspot to see the risk described in three parts: the hazard, the event, and the consequence, including who's at risk. Before you open each one, try to describe it yourself.
