Escalate and Explain Your Choice

~1 min · narrated

Escalate

  • No control found for a very high or high risk? Consult the contracting PCBU (the client).
  • The client can’t contract out of risk management – they may need to change the specification or contract terms.

Explain

  • Document your reasoning for the contractor PCBU, contracting PCBU and TAO.
  • Check each risk’s reduction and the lowest total risk – if either fails, select again.
NZGTTM

“If the reasoning cannot clearly be explained, then the planning process has failed.”

Two last points on selecting controls. First, escalation. If controls can't be found for very high or high risks, the contractor PCBU must consult the contracting PCBU, the client. The client can't contract out of risk management and must help reduce the risk, which might mean changing the project specification or the contract terms. Second, explain your choice. Planners must be clear in their reasoning and document it for the contractor PCBU, the contracting PCBU and the transport authority. The NZGTTM puts it bluntly: if the reasoning cannot clearly be explained, then the planning process has failed. Once controls are chosen, check both the reduction for each risk and the lowest total risk. If either fails, go back and select again.
