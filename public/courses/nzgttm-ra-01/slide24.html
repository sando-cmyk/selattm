The Hierarchy of Controls

~1 min · narrated

Choosing controls is as important as identifying risks – and not all controls are equally effective. The Health and Safety at Work (General Risk and Workplace Management) Regulations 2016, reg 6 sets the order that must be applied.

OrderControlWhat it means
1 – most effectiveEliminateRemove the risk, so far as is reasonably practicable
2Substitute, isolate, engineerMinimise the risk with one or more of these – the most appropriate and effective
3AdministrativeIf risk remains, minimise it further
4 – least effectivePPEOnly for the risk that is left
REMEMBER

Hi-vis clothing is important – but it’s the least effective control you have.

Module four: selecting controls and lowest total risk. Once you've identified and rated the risks, you have to decide how to eliminate or minimise them. Not all controls are equally effective. The Health and Safety at Work regulations set out the hierarchy of control measures, and it must be applied in order. First, eliminate the risk, so far as is reasonably practicable. If you can't, minimise it by substituting, isolating, or using engineering controls, choosing whichever are most appropriate and effective. If risk remains, minimise it with administrative controls. And only then, for whatever is left, use personal protective equipment. Hi-vis clothing matters, but it's the least effective control you have.
