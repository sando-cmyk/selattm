Worked Example: Footpath Closure

~1 min · narrated

A utility crew repairs a cabinet on a verge beside a busy two-lane suburban arterial. The base case: close the footpath – room for the crew, and the public kept away from live cables.

But total risk includes…

  • Public reaching live cables
  • Pedestrians crossing where drivers don’t expect them
  • Drivers swerving to avoid pedestrians
  • Workers hit by an out-of-control vehicle

Likely lower total risk

  • A temporary footpath past the work
  • Guiding pedestrians through the site with appropriate staffing
SOUND FAMILIAR?

This is the same kind of site as the Module 2 and 3 activities.

The second worked example is one you'll recognise from earlier modules. A utility crew is repairing a cabinet on a verge, with a footpath past it and a busy two-lane suburban arterial alongside. The obvious answer is to close the footpath: it gives the technicians room and keeps the public away from live cables. But there's no other footpath or verge. So pedestrians will cross the road where drivers aren't expecting them. Total risk here is the risk from the live cables, plus the risk to pedestrians crossing, plus the risk to drivers swerving to avoid them, plus the risk to workers from an out of control vehicle. A temporary footpath, or staff guiding pedestrians through the site, is likely to give a lower total risk.
