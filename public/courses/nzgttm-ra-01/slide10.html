ACTIVITY CONTEXT

What the client is doing, how, when and for how long – their intentions and goals.

Environment context – five categories:

  • Transport corridor – lanes, bus and cycle lanes, footpaths, intersections, traffic signals, street lighting.
  • Programme – the days and times of the activity.
  • Network users – everyone who may be affected, across all modes.
  • Neighbours – owners and users of property affected by the activity.
  • Natural environment – weather, or natural hazards such as cliff faces.
The first step in planning any site is to clearly understand the activity and its environment. Activity context means understanding what the client is actually doing, how they're doing it, when, and for how long. Environment context has five categories. The transport corridor: the lanes, bus lanes, cycle lanes, footpaths, intersections, signals and street lighting your activity will affect. The programme: the days and times of the work. Network users: everyone who may be affected, across every mode. Neighbours: the owners and users of property affected by the activity. And the natural environment, such as weather, or natural hazards like cliff faces. Contractor PCBUs need to describe this context alongside the activity before they can properly consider the risks.
