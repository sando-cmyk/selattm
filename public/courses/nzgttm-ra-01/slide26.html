Lowest Total Risk

~1 min · narrated

A control for one risk can increase or transfer risk to another group, or introduce a new risk.

EXAMPLE

Closing a road removes the risk to workers – but if road users are detoured onto a much less safe road, one group’s risk has simply moved to another. The total risk may not have gone down at all.

Total risk = the change in risk for every group affected

  • Workers and event participants
  • Drivers, passengers, cyclists, pedestrians, public transport users
  • Local residents and businesses
DON’T TRANSFER RISK

Transferring risk from one group to another – for example from workers to the public – is unethical and inconsistent with HSWA 2015.

Sometimes a control for one risk increases or transfers risk to another group of people, or introduces a new risk altogether. That's where lowest total risk comes in. In transport, many different people are affected by the same decision. Closing a road to protect workers might send road users onto a detour that's much less safe. One group's risk goes down while another's goes up, so the total risk may not reduce at all. The NZGTTM is clear: it's unethical, and inconsistent with the Health and Safety at Work Act, to transfer risk from one group to another, for example from workers to the public. Always weigh up the total.
