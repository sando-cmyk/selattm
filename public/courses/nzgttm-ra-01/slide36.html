THE TRAP

If advance warning signs are spaced only for safe stopping distance and the queue is longer, there’s no warning of the stopped queue – an uncontrolled risk.

TTM is permanent road design delivered temporarily, so simple traffic engineering checks back up your risk assessment. A single lane can carry about eighteen hundred vehicles an hour, because at maximum density vehicles are about two seconds apart, and an hour is thirty-six hundred seconds. Traffic signals and stop-go only give each direction part of the hour, so capacity is lower. Stopped traffic queues. If one thousand vehicles an hour are stopped for forty seconds, and each vehicle takes about eight metres, that's a queue of about eighty-nine metres. And when demand is higher than capacity, the queue keeps growing. Fifteen hundred vehicles an hour arriving at a merge that can only take thirteen hundred builds two hundred queued vehicles every hour. After two hours, that's around three point two kilometres. Here's why it matters: if your advance warning signs are spaced only for safe stopping distance, and the queue is longer than that, drivers meet the back of the queue with no warning. That's an uncontrolled risk.
