What a TMP Contains, and Who Reads It

~1 min · narrated

The STMS is the most important audience – they set up the site and manage the real-world risks.

Who’s it for

  • Contracting PCBU and TAO
  • STMS and work crews
  • Assurers and reviewers

What it includes

  • Reference, approval, organisations, contacts
  • Activity and environment context
  • Risk register – controls and residual risks
  • Consultation record and communication plan
  • TTM details – time, location, plant, equipment
  • Contingency plans
  • Quality, assurance and control; peer reviews
  • Diagrams
IN COURT

If anybody is hurt, the TMP – with any amendments – will be used in court to show how the site was set out.

A TMP has several audiences: the contracting PCBU, the transport authority, the STMS, work crews and assurers. The most important person to communicate the risks and the site design to is the STMS, because they set up the site and manage the real-world risks to workers and road users. So a TMP needs to include: the TMP reference and approval; the organisations involved and contact details; a project overview with the activity and environment context; the risk register, with controls and residual risks; a record of who was consulted; the communication plan; the proposed TTM details, meaning time, location, plant and equipment; contingency plans for when something changes or goes wrong; the quality, assurance and control approach; peer reviews; and diagrams. And keep in mind: if anybody is hurt, the TMP, with any amendments, will be used in court to show how the site was set out.
