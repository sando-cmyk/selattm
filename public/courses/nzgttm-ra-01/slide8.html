Hazard, Exposure, Risk

~1 min · narrated

You can’t control a risk you haven’t found. Start with three words the NZGTTM uses throughout.

HHazard

Something that can cause harm.

EExposure

To cause harm, somebody must interact with the hazard.

RRisk

The chance the hazard will cause harm, and how severe that harm could be.
NZGTTM EXAMPLE

A washout on the shoulder of a road is a hazard. People travelling down the road will interact with it – exposure. There’s a risk of possible harm to some people.

Module two: context and identifying risks. You can't control a risk you haven't found. So before we look at controls, we need to be clear on three words. A hazard is something that can cause harm. Exposure is when somebody interacts with that hazard. And risk is the chance that the hazard will cause harm, together with how bad that harm could be. The NZGTTM uses a simple example. A washout on the shoulder of a road is a hazard. People driving along that road are exposed to it. So there's a risk of harm to some of those people. Take away the exposure, and the hazard is still there, but the risk to people changes. Keep these three words in mind for the rest of the course.
