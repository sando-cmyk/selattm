Activity: Choose the Control

~2 min (interactive) · narrated

For each site, pick a fundamental methodology (around, through, past, in the gaps) and say why. Then tap to compare with the model answer.

0 of 3 explored

A: Rural 100 km/h edge repair

Site: One day of seal-edge repair on a two-lane rural highway. The nearest alternative route adds 40 km.

Model answer – go past: a lane closure with an exclusion zone and a TMA protecting the crew, plus a temporary speed limit only through the work area.

Total risk: a 40 km detour on rural roads would move risk onto the public. If the lane can’t fit an exclusion zone, consider go through (stop/go or portable signals).

B: Residential water main

Site: Two days of trench work across a quiet residential street, with a short, safe route around the block.

Model answer – go around: close the street and detour traffic around the block, eliminating traffic from the site.

Total risk: the detour is short and safe, but keep pedestrian and resident access, and allow for emergency vehicles.

C: Footpath outside a school

Site: Half a day of footpath repair outside a primary school gate on a 50 km/h road.

Model answer – timing + a protected temporary path: work outside drop-off and pick-up times, and give pedestrians a protected temporary path rather than closing the footpath.

Total risk: closing the footpath would send children across the road – a higher total risk than the work itself.

THERE’S RARELY ONE RIGHT ANSWER

What matters is that your choice follows the hierarchy, passes the lowest-total-risk test, and you can explain it.

Time to choose. For each of these three sites, decide which fundamental methodology you'd use, around, through, past or in the gaps, and why. Think about lowest total risk for everyone affected. Then tap each site to compare with the model answer.
