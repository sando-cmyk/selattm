The Risk Register

~1 min · narrated

The risk register documents each step of the risk assessment and lets everyone in the contracting chain communicate about it. People won’t always agree on risk levels – that’s why it matters.

The register should contain
Risks identified – hazard, event, consequence
Assessment – a ranking of each risk
Control options – the fundamental TTM controls in preferred order
Residual risk – what’s left if each option were used
Lowest total risk – how each option works for road users and workers
Adopted controls – what will actually be implemented
Peer review – reviewer, feedback, and the contractor PCBU’s response and decision
WHY IT MATTERS

The documented decision is a critical record should the worst happen and somebody gets hurt.

Module five: the risk register, the TMP and review. The risk register documents each step of your risk assessment, and it's how everyone in the contracting chain communicates about risk. Not everybody will agree on the levels of risk, and that's natural, which is exactly why the register matters. It should contain the risks identified, described as the hazard, the event and the consequence. The assessment of each risk. The possible control options, using the fundamental TTM controls in preferred order. The residual risk left if each option was used. A lowest total risk assessment for each option. And the control options actually adopted. Feedback and decisions from peer review belong in it too. Remember: the documented decision is a critical record should the worst happen and somebody gets hurt.
