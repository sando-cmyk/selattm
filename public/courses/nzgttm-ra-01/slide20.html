Reading the Ranking

~1 min · narrated

Once every risk has a rating, rank them so the highest-rated risks are dealt with first.

  • Not all risks are equal. Find the highest-rated risks and focus there first.
  • Look at both the absolute rating and the relative differences between risks.
  • The highest-ranked risks get priority for elimination, or for controls that minimise them.
WHO DECIDES

The contracting PCBU decides the best way to assess the risks and makes sure the assessment team is an appropriately diverse group. People will rate the same risk differently – that conversation is the point.

Once each risk has a rating, rank them. Not all risks are equal. The goal is to find the risks with the highest ratings, so you can focus on those first, either eliminating them or developing controls to minimise them. Look at both the absolute rating and the relative differences between risks. Two risks might both be rated high, but one may be much closer to very high than the other. It's the contracting PCBU's responsibility to decide the best way to assess risks, and to make sure the risk assessment team is an appropriately diverse group of people. Different people will rate the same risk differently, which is exactly why the conversation matters.
