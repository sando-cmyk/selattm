Installing and Removing the Site

~1 min · narrated

Installing

  • In the TMP, and risk assessed like the active site
  • Install as designed – e.g. a TMA positioned to protect workers, a barrier with its end treatment
  • Mark any changes on the TMP so it stays an accurate record

Removing – often high risk

  • Fatigue, and crews focused on getting home
  • Risk assess it first: duration, length, complexity, permanent speed, observed speeds, time of day, weather and glare
  • Plan shift changes; removing a long-term site can confuse regular drivers
Installing the site should be included in the TMP and risk assessed in the same way as the active site. If it isn't installed as designed, the controls may not work. For example, a TMA not positioned to protect workers, or a barrier missing its end treatment. Any changes made during set-up must be marked on the TMP so it stays an accurate record. Removing the site is often high risk, because crews are fatigued and focused on getting home. So the removal process should be in the TMP and risk assessed too. Before removal, consider the duration and length of the site, its complexity, the permanent speed of the road, the speeds actually observed during the work, the time of day, and conditions like weather or sun glare. Removing a long-term site can even confuse regular drivers.
