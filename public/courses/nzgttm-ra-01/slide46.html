THE EMERGENCY VISION

“Do the best you can with what you have, to ensure road workers and road users go home safe every day.”

PhaseRoughlyWhat it looks like
Short termFirst hourReactive, limited resources
Medium term1–3 hoursStill reactive; more resources arrive
Long term3 hours+More leadership; changes made in a planned way
RecoveryUntil normalNormal operation with pre-planning and peer review

Move to a planned response as quickly as possible. This doesn’t apply to work that can be foreseen and planned, like routine maintenance.

Emergencies are different. Crashes, fires, floods, live power cables, gas leaks and large objects on the road may not allow time for a full risk assessment and TMP. But PCBUs are still legally obliged to keep workers and the public safe, so far as is reasonably practicable. So the vision changes to: do the best you can with what you have, to ensure road workers and road users go home safe every day. Responders should cause no more harm, whether through action or inaction. There are four phases: the short-term response, roughly the first hour; the medium term, up to around three hours; the long term, until the incident is stabilised; and the recovery period, back to normal operation with full planning. The aim is to move to a planned response as quickly as possible. And this approach doesn't apply to work that can be foreseen and planned, like routine maintenance.
