Practical Design Rules

~1 min · narrated

Space and time

  • Keep the site short – reduce speed only where it’s needed
  • Time it from actual hourly counts; if traffic is still heavy after a crash, wait
  • Set up just before, pack up straight after

People and network

  • Minimise blocked lanes – can the vehicle use the shoulder or verge?
  • Realistic options for walking and cycling – long diversions won’t be used
  • Coordinate with other works nearby
BEFORE YOU CHOOSE

Consult the people who will implement the controls, the people doing the works, and groups representing road users.

A few practical rules make any control work better. Keep the length of road and path under TTM as short as possible. Long sites where drivers can't see why they're being delayed lead to speeding, so only reduce speeds where it's needed. Choose your timing from actual hourly traffic counts, and if a crash means traffic is still heavy, wait until it clears. Set up right before the activity starts and pack up as soon as it's finished. Don't block a lane if the work vehicle can use the shoulder or verge. Give pedestrians and cyclists realistic options, because a long diversion won't be used. And coordinate with other work nearby. Before choosing, consult the people who'll implement it and the groups who represent road users.
