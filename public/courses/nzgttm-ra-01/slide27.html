Worked Example: SH6 Culvert

~1.5 min · narrated

A culvert on SH6, about 2 km south of the SH63 junction (Nelson–Westport) needs replacing. The road is 7 m seal-to-seal – too narrow to separate workers from passing traffic.

OptionTotal-risk verdict
Full closure – 360 km, 5-hour detourTransfers risk to the public – likely not acceptable
Closure with periodic openings (e.g. 50 min closed, 10 min open)Eliminates worker risk, public stay on route – total risk reduction
Night worksReduction, but adds fatigue, poor visibility and cold
Pipe jacking (substitute)Potential reduction – needs its own risk assessment
Barrier (isolate)Reduction – needs very small deflection on a narrow road
Speed measures (engineering)Small reduction – doesn’t separate workers
Administrative / PPE aloneVery little reduction
COST IS NOT A REASON

The higher cost of a longer job is not a justifiable reason to avoid a better control (HSWA 2015).

Here's the NZGTTM's first worked example. A culvert needs replacing on State Highway six, about two kilometres south of the State Highway sixty-three junction, between Nelson and Westport. The road is seven metres wide with no room for a safe exclusion zone. Closing the road would eliminate risk to workers, but the only detour is three hundred and sixty kilometres and five hours longer. That's a transfer of risk to the public, and probably not acceptable. So look again at the hierarchy. Closing the road for fifty minutes, then opening it for ten, still eliminates risk to workers while they're on the road, and keeps the public on the same route with an inconvenient, but well communicated, delay. Night works, pipe jacking, a narrow-deflection barrier, and speed reduction measures were all weighed up too. Administrative controls and PPE do very little on their own. The recommended option: closure with periodic openings. And remember: the extra cost of a longer job is not a justifiable reason to avoid a better control.
