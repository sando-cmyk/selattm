The Five Fundamental TTM Methodologies

~1.5 min · narrated

The hierarchy of controls, applied to TTM – in order of preference:

#MethodOptionsWatch out for
1Remove the need to do the work—Usually not possible – but always ask
2Go around the siteDetour, temporary roadDon’t push road users, pedestrians or cyclists onto a less safe route
3Go through the sitePortable traffic control device, manual stop/stop or stop/goRoad users’ risk passing through. A device beats exposing a person
4Go past the siteBarrier or exclusion zone, TMA, stop/go periodsBarriers need end treatments; deflection must suit the space
5In the gaps – lowestSafe work method statements; training and close supervisionNeeds a contingency if a worker can’t clear the lane in time
The NZGTTM applies the hierarchy of controls to TTM as five fundamental methodologies, in order of preference. One: remove the need to do the work. If the work isn't needed, there's no risk to anyone. Two: go around the site, with a detour or a temporary road, so traffic is removed from the worksite. Take care not to send road users, pedestrians or cyclists onto a less safe route. Three: go through the site. The road is temporarily closed so workers aren't exposed, then the work pauses and traffic is released through. That can use a portable traffic control device, which is better than putting a person at risk, or manual stop-go. Four: go past the site, separating traffic from workers with a barrier or exclusion zone, a truck mounted attenuator, or stop-go periods. Five, the lowest form: in the gaps, where workers enter the lane between vehicles. That relies on safe work method statements, training and close supervision, and it needs a contingency plan for when a worker can't get off the road in time.
