Newton’s Third Law: Action and Reaction

~1 min · narrated

For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. When a ute hits a worker, the force on the ute and the force on the worker are exactly the same. So why does the ute barely slow down while the worker is thrown?

Because the same force on a much smaller mass produces a much bigger change in speed (the Second Law again).

YOU WILL ALWAYS LOSE THE MASS CONTEST

High-vis clothing helps a driver see you sooner. It doesn’t change the physics once contact happens. Stay where a vehicle can’t reach you: behind barriers, inside the work area, away from the live lane edge, and facing traffic.

PUTTING MASS ON YOUR SIDE

A shadow vehicle works as a heavy blocker between traffic and the crew. If a car hits it, the car comes off second best, and the truck absorbs the car’s momentum instead of a worker. The truck will still be shunted forward, so the TMP leaves a roll-ahead gap between it and the crew.

Newton's third law: for every action, there's an equal and opposite reaction. When a ute hits a worker, the force on the ute and the force on the worker are exactly the same. So why does the ute keep going while the worker is thrown? Because the same force on a much smaller mass gives a much bigger change in speed. A twenty-two hundred kilo ute is about twenty-seven times heavier than an eighty kilo worker. At fifty kilometres an hour, the ute slows by about two kilometres an hour. The worker is accelerated to about forty-eight. You will always lose the mass contest. High-vis helps drivers see you, but it can't change the physics. Your protection is to be somewhere a vehicle can't reach. A heavy shadow truck, acting as a blocker, puts that mass difference on your side.
