FOLLOW THE PLAN

This course explains why controls exist. Site layouts, distances and speeds come from the approved TMP and the NZGTTM risk assessment. Cones guide traffic but won’t stop a vehicle.

Every temporary traffic management control does at least one of three things. It lowers speed, it adds time and distance, or it keeps the vehicle's energy away from people. A temporary speed limit cuts energy, which depends on speed squared, and shortens stopping distance. Advance warning gives drivers their reaction time earlier. Safety zones give an errant vehicle room to stop before it reaches anyone. A heavy shadow truck acts as a blocker, taking the errant vehicle's momentum before it reaches the crew ahead. The attenuator on the back of that truck is there for the road user: it crushes to stretch out their stop, so hitting the blocker is survivable for them too. Barriers redirect a vehicle's momentum. Secured loads and seatbelts deal with inertia. And facing traffic buys you reaction time. Remember, cones guide traffic. They won't stop a vehicle. Always follow the approved plan.
