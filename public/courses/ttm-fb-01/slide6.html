Speed Squared: Kinetic Energy

~1 min · narrated

Every moving vehicle carries kinetic energy. In a crash all of it is released in a fraction of a second, into bending steel, breaking glass and breaking bodies.

THE FORMULA

Kinetic energy = ½ × mass × speed²

Because speed is squared, double the speed means four times the energy. A good way to feel the numbers is to compare being hit with falling from a height.

ON SITE

Bringing traffic down from 100 to 50 km/h past a work area removes about three-quarters of the energy that could reach your crew.

Every moving vehicle carries kinetic energy, and in a crash it's all released in a fraction of a second. Kinetic energy equals half the mass times the speed squared. Because speed is squared, small changes in speed make a big difference. Double the speed, and you get four times the energy. One way to feel this is to compare being hit with falling. Being hit at thirty is like falling off a single-storey roof. At fifty, it's like falling from a three-storey building. At a hundred, it's a thirteen-storey fall. So when a temporary speed limit brings traffic down from a hundred to fifty past your site, it removes about three-quarters of the energy that could reach your crew.
