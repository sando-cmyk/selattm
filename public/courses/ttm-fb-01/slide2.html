Newton’s First Law: Inertia

~1 min · narrated

Newton’s First Law of Motion states: “A body at rest will remain at rest, and a body in motion will remain in motion, unless it is acted upon by an external force.”

A body in motion…

  • A vehicle travelling at 100 km/h wants to keep going at 100 km/h.
  • So does everything inside it: the driver, passengers, tools and loose gear.
  • Brakes, a seatbelt or a crash are the “external force”.

A body at rest…

  • A worker standing on the roadside has zero speed.
  • If a vehicle hits them, the body has to be pushed from 0 to vehicle speed in a few hundredths of a second.
  • That sudden push is what causes the injury.
ON SITE

Unsecured cones, signs and tools on a ute deck or shadow truck keep moving when the vehicle stops. A loose sign base becomes a projectile. Secure every load, and never ride on the deck or tail of a moving vehicle.

Newton's first law is about inertia. A body at rest stays at rest, and a body in motion stays in motion, unless a force acts on it. On the road, that means a vehicle at a hundred kilometres an hour wants to keep doing a hundred, and so does everything inside it: the driver, the passengers, and any loose gear. Brakes, a seatbelt, or a crash supply the force that changes that. It works the other way too. A worker standing on the roadside isn't moving. If a vehicle hits them, their body has to be pushed from standing still to vehicle speed in a fraction of a second. That sudden push is what injures. And remember, unsecured gear on a site vehicle keeps moving when the vehicle stops.
