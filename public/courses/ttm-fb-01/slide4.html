THE FORMULA

Force = mass × change in speed ÷ stopping time

You can’t change a person’s mass, and once a crash starts you can’t change the speed. The only thing left is the stopping time. Halve the time and you double the force.

ON SITE

Crumple zones, seatbelts, airbags and the truck-mounted attenuator (TMA) on the back of a shadow vehicle all protect the people inside a crashing vehicle the same way: they stretch the stop over more time and distance. A worker on foot has none of these.

Newton's second law says force equals mass times acceleration. In a crash, that acceleration depends on two things: how much the speed changes, and how long the stop takes. You can't change a person's mass, and once a crash has started you can't change the speed. The only thing left is time. Halve the stopping time and you double the force. Look at the table. A person stopping from fifty against something rigid stops in about five thousandths of a second, and takes a force of over two hundred kilonewtons. Spread the same stop over a tenth of a second with a belt and crumple zone, and the force drops to about eleven. That's why crumple zones, seatbelts, and the attenuator on the back of a shadow truck all work by stretching out the stop for the people inside a crashing vehicle.
