Stopping Distance: Can They Stop in Time?

~1 min (interactive) · narrated

A driver who sees your crew can’t stop instantly. First they react, then the brakes remove the vehicle’s kinetic energy. Total stopping distance = reaction distance + braking distance. Braking distance grows with the square of speed.

Try it: can the driver stop before reaching the crew?

A driver who sees your crew can't stop instantly. First they have to react, and in a second and a half at seventy kilometres an hour, they've already travelled almost thirty metres without even touching the brakes. Then the brakes have to remove the vehicle's kinetic energy, and because that energy depends on speed squared, the braking distance does too. At fifty, a car needs about thirty-five metres to stop. At a hundred, almost a hundred metres. On a wet road or loose chip it's longer again. Use the calculator to try different speeds, surfaces and distances, and see how fast the vehicle would still be going when it reached the crew.
