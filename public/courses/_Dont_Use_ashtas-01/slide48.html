Nuts and locking threads

When tightening a fastener set to the correct torque, normally with a torque wrench, the measurement is applied to the nut not the head of the screw or bolt. In the same way, the orientation of the fastener is critical to performance. Under most circumstances the nut will be located to the non-traffic face. The strength of the material used will be specified on the delivery note. This is to match the system owner's specification.

Nuts can be used singularly, tightened to a given torque value or with a locking insert to prevent loosening. In the same manner a second nut, this may be a full or half nut, may be tightened against the main nut to lock it in place. If this is done, the main nut is to be torqued to the correct value and then held whilst the second nut is tightened against it. It must be ensured that at least one full thread projects through the second or locking nut.

Fasteners with forged flanges are becoming more popular. The flange replaces the need for a washer. Often the flange is serrated on the contact face to prevent loosening. These nuts are often slightly smaller due to the material used in manufacture and it must be ensured that the correct socket is available for tightening.

The final option is to chemically bond the nut to the thread of the fastener. This may be achieved by application of a liquid threadloc, or the screw or bolt may have an application of a dry two-part resin that is mixed by the screwing action of the nut. If these type of bonding agents are used, they should only be utilised under system owners' direction, and it should be noted that torque requirements may have to be adjusted.

Above all else, only use the nut specified by the system owner.
