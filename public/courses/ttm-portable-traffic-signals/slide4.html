Why "Portable Traffic Signals"?
Menu
Let's start with terminology, because it matters more than it sounds. The correct industry term is Portable Traffic Signals, not temporary lights or portable traffic lights. That term comes directly from the Land Transport Rule: Traffic Control Devices, which is the legal basis for every sign, marking, and signal used on a New Zealand road — and it classifies this equipment as a traffic signal. NZGTTM, the New Zealand Guide to Temporary Traffic Management, uses Portable Traffic Signals as its standard term throughout. The word portable simply describes how the unit is deployed and relocated on site — it doesn't change its legal status or how seriously it needs to be treated. Using the correct, defined term keeps your Traffic Management Plan, your Works Access Permit, and your site paperwork consistent with the documents NZTA and road controlling authorities actually work from. Casual language might seem harmless, but it can create real ambiguity in incident reports, TMPs, and audits — so from here on, we'll use Portable Traffic Signals throughout this course.
