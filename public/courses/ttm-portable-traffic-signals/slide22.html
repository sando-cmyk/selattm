Fail-Safe Behaviour (AS 4191)
Menu
It's important to get fail-safe behaviour right, because this is a common area of confusion. On detecting a communication loss, lamp failure, power failure, or internal fault, AS 4191 requires the system to default to one of two safe states. The standard, and most common, fail-safe is flashing amber, or yellow — and this is deliberate: it's exactly the same fail-safe behaviour used by permanent traffic signals across New Zealand, so road users already recognise it and know to proceed with caution. The accepted alternative is an all-off state, where the signal goes fully dark and a TTM Worker takes over with manual Stop-Go control until the fault is fixed. Either way, this fail-safe behaviour is a strict AS 4191 requirement, and it must be tested — not just assumed — as part of your daily inspection.
