By their nature posts are not normally used with rigid systems, but there are exceptions. The post defined by the system owner must be used when installing the Barrier System. They cannot be interchanged as there will be no guarantee of performance if a non-compliant system is installed. The profile of the post will affect the way it performs.

Generally, there are two types of post; strong and weak. The type of post will be dependent on its profile, performance will be different even if the post fits into the same size box; for example, a one hundred and ten by seventy-millimetre Z-post, which is a weak post, will perform totally differently to a one hundred and ten by seventy-millimetre Sigma Post, a strong post.

Other post profiles are used in addition to the ones shown including a square post and a U-post. The core difference between strong and weak post systems is how they work. A weak post system is designed to fail, normally by folding, at ground level. The surrounding foundation is stronger than the post. A strong post system is designed such that the post rotates from its base disrupting the foundation or failing to collapse.
The latter generally creates two issues. A vehicles wheel can catch on the post resulting in the vehicle spinning or flipping, so strong post systems often have block-outs to prevent this from happening. The second is that as the post has a high level of resistance the occupant impact severity can be higher than the same profile rail with a weak post attached.

The primary purpose of the post is to assist in keeping the rail at the correct height during an impact. If the post pulls the rail down the vehicle can go over it and impact the hazard, or if the rail is permitted to rise the vehicle may pass under it having the top of the cab removed. As a result, with weak post systems where the post is designed to fail, the fastener connecting the post and rail is designed to fail during impact, so over or under torqueing of fasteners may be a cause of system failure.

Posts may be driven, if the soil conditions are suitable or set in socketed foundations or surface mounted. It is essential that the designer’s instructions are fully complied with particularly on structures where the impact forces may be an issue if transferred into the structure. In some cases, the fasteners may be designed to fail before the structure fails. Again, over or under torqueing of fasteners may be a cause of system failure.

For those continuing to the installer training this is discussed further then.

If required by the system design block-outs must be fitted, but block-outs cannot be used to correct issues of post alignment.
