Total Quality Management and is a system for focusing on the Customer. It feeds into all aspects of the Plan Do Check Act cycle and is reflected within the attitude of all those participating in the quality activity. The advantage of using the Total Quality Management approach is it delivers ‘right-first-time, right-every-time’ customer focused activities.

So, who are your customers? If you are the Supervisor or Leading Hand giving the Start of Shift Briefing, the people you are briefing are your customers. If it is clear and concise it will be over quickly and everybody will know what has to be done and by whom. If the briefing rambles on there is the potential for confusion or information being missed.

In the same way, if you move a traffic management sign that is in your way, what effect does that have on the overall traffic management? Is the sign still providing the correct information to the travelling public? If there was an accident could moving the sign be part of the problem? That is why traffic management is moved by trained traffic management personnel, they understand the consequences of their actions and they know their plan.

If we consider the customer list for a project from the top down the Road Authority is the customer of the Designer as they will receive the project but so are the installation team as they receive the design to implement.

In the cycle shown all the participants are each other’s customers and each other’s suppliers, it is purely that some are physical transactions whilst some are informational transactions. By considering the supply chain in this manner a better flow of information is achieved leading to a better product and process.

The benefits of Total Quality Management are normally measured as quality measurements of Quality, Cost and Delivery.

When thinking of quality, we could consider no rework, this saves time and materials, the time you are at risk on road works is reduced. If we then carry that through to cost, if the job time is reduced and there are no additional costs, the employer remains competitive or even improves their quality profile winning more work. The overall project cost is contained as the works are completed quicker; and finally by delivering in this way, the road user is happier as the disruption is shorter.

It is noted that this is a very simplistic model and viewpoint; it can be far more complex if you consider the number of times the installation crew and deliveries pull in and out of site interacting with the travelling public which is a high risk activity or the wastage from having to re-pour foundations.
