Within the ASHTAS framework competency is defined using the acronym SKATE.

Skate refers to Skills, Knowledge, Attitude, Training and Experience

Skills considers how the job is done and the ability of the person to do the job, this may involve having other work-based qualifications or as simple as being able to read a drawing correctly.

Knowledge considers what is known, again not only about the product directly, but why things are done correctly, note the argument that ‘it has always been done this way’ is not knowledge, it is the understanding of Skills and Training that gives knowledge.

Attitude, this is often demonstrated through logbooks as the willingness to do things right because they are to be done correctly, not to pass the test. The right-first-time approach is a demonstration of positive attitude.

Training, the study of products and processes through learning. This can include on-the-job training as well as formal training. Training gives rise to knowledge over time.

Experience is time based. It is the application of the Skills, Knowledge, Attitude and Training successfully over time.

It must be noted using this framework that you are not competent when you have trained; competency is open-ended and as you continue to develop so does your competency.

The ASHTAS framework defines competency at different stages, at Operative stage, at Installer stage and for each individual product groups. It isn’t defined solely by time on the job.
