Barrier System Types Summary & Considerations

Rigid Barrier Systems normally result in high impact severity on the occupants and the vehicle. They will contain higher energy impacts, for example large vehicles and have low deflections when impacted.

Generally, they require little or no maintenance and repair from smaller impacts, although large impacts may require rebuilding or removal and replacement.

Semi-rigid barrier systems often have lower occupant impact severity level than rigid systems and correspondingly less vehicle damage. This does not necessarily mean that the vehicle will be able to be driven post impact. Semi-ridged systems are designed to operate in different ways and therefore posts are not interchangeable.

Due to the design, it is often the case that both the rail and the posts impacted will require replacement.
Steel systems may be recycled.

Flexible barrier systems often have the lowest occupant severity levels. Due to the low impact severity the vehicle may have less structural damage. It must be noted that the wire rope may cut into an impacting wing. As these systems have less severity on the occupants, the system deflections are larger from impacts, so may be unsuitable for use in areas with hazards located close to the highway.
