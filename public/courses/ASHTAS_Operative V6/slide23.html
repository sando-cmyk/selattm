Whilst the MASH test criteria are not something you need to be aware of as an operative, it is interesting to understand how barrier systems are tested. The tables shown details the MASH test criteria for each of 6 test levels. The test does not look at all possible forms of impact but provides a representative sample of the worst practical conditions based on speed and angle of impact. It also looks at the type of vehicle to be used in the test specifying different vehicles for different test levels. The test does not consider aquaplaning, road ice, illegal activities including speeding and assumes the barrier is fit for purpose – not rusty or damaged.

The test also looks at Critical Impact Points which means they look at the weakest point in the installation where the vehicle may burst through or pass over the installed system and the strongest point in the system where the occupant may receive the worst damage due to excessive stopping or trapping.

The test level represents where the road barrier is designed to be used. Test levels one and two would be used for residential and urban environments. Test level three for freeway, expressway or motorway. Test levels for to six also include the use of a truck in addition to the small car and pickup this is where the barrier is designed to provide asset protection for example a bridge pier.

The Dodge Ram 1500 is the only practical vehicle that can be used at present in the tests replacing the 2270P Pick up.

The containment specification will always be specified by the road authority.
