Excavations and Barriers

~2 min · narrated
THE 2 : 1 RULE

Widen the lateral exclusion zone by 2 × the excavation depth.
500 mm deep → +1.0 m  ·  800 mm deep → +1.6 m

Why

  • Trench edges can collapse under a wheel
  • A drifting vehicle needs somewhere to go

Consider a barrier when

  • You can’t fit the width
  • The excavation is deep or open for a long time
  • Remember barrier deflection, end treatments and an installation designer
Excavations need extra width. The NZGTTM uses an angle of repose approach: widen the lateral exclusion zone by two units for every unit of depth. So a trench five hundred millimetres deep adds one metre. A trench eight hundred millimetres deep adds 1.6 metres. That keeps the edge of the trench from collapsing under a vehicle and gives a drifting wheel somewhere to go. If you can't fit that width, or the excavation is deep, the risk is high and a temporary road safety barrier should be on the table. Remember from the risk assessment course: barriers sit high in the hierarchy of controls, because they physically stop the vehicle. But a barrier needs its own deflection space, the right end treatments and an installation designer. So it's a design decision, not just a swap.
