Rural 100 km/h, approach

Design the advance warning to 100 km/h (2.5 s group). Drivers haven’t slowed yet.

Inside a stop/go site

Traffic released from the stop/go is slow, so a lower operating speed can be used past the work area – if you can show it’s achieved.

TSL sign only

Don’t assume the temporary limit. A sign alone often doesn’t slow traffic. Design to the speed you expect (survey it), or add measures that achieve the lower speed.

Reaction time is really four steps, called PIEV. Perception: you notice something ahead. Identification: you work out it's a cone, a sign or a worker. Emotion: how you feel about it. Volition: you decide to act. Only then do you brake or steer. The NZGTTM assumes reaction time grows with speed, because lower-speed roads tend to be busier and more complex, which keeps drivers more alert. So there are three groups: 1.5 seconds for 30 to 50 kilometres an hour, 2 seconds for 60 to 80, and 2.5 seconds for 90 to 110. These three groups come up again and again in this course. For people on foot, the NZGTTM uses a conservative walking speed of 1.2 metres per second. Now try the activity. Tap each site to see which speed you'd design to.
