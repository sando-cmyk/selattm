≈ 0.9 m30–50 km/h
≈ 1.2 m60–80 km/h
≈ 1.5 m90–110 km/h

0.6 m/s drift × reaction time. Check the NZGTTM table for the values to use.

WHAT’S BEYOND THE ZONE?

A swerving driver crosses it faster and won’t stop at the edge. The bigger the hazard beyond – workers, plant, a drop-off – the wider the zone.

The lateral exclusion zone is the sideways buffer between the line of cones and the work area. It allows for a driver who drifts out of their lane. The NZGTTM assumes a gentle drift of 0.6 metres per second over the reaction time, which gives about 0.9 metres in the 30 to 50 group, 1.2 metres at 60 to 80, and 1.5 metres at 90 to 110. But a driver who swerves will cross that zone much faster, and won't necessarily stop at the edge of your work area. So ask: what's on the other side? Fresh seal at the same level is one thing. Workers on foot, a trench, or a parked excavator is another. The greater the hazard beyond the zone, the wider it should be.
