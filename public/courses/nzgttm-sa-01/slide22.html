Evidence That Stands Up

Your report may be read by the STMS, the client, the RCA, WorkSafe – or a court.

  • Time-stamped and located – chainage, landmark or GPS on every photo
  • Wide, then close – context first, then the defect
  • Numbered and referred to in each finding
  • Who said what – names and times of conversations
  • Privacy – no faces unless needed as evidence

Your report may be read by the STMS, the client, the road controlling authority, WorkSafe, or even a court. So your evidence has to stand up. Every photo should be time-stamped and located. Take a wide shot for context, then a close-up of the defect. Number your photos and refer to them in your findings. Write down who told you what, and when. Avoid photographing faces unless you need to. And never stand in the traffic path to get a photo. Use a dashcam or a longer lens instead.
