The Hierarchy of Controls in TTM

~1 min · narrated
LevelControlTTM examples
1 – most effectiveEliminateDetour traffic away; full closure; work when there is no traffic
2SubstitutePortable traffic signals instead of a controller at the roadside
3IsolateRated barriers between traffic and workers; separated pedestrian corridor
4EngineerTruck-mounted attenuator; lane alignment; speed management devices
5AdministrativeTSL, signs, safe work methods, briefings, supervision
6 – least effectivePPEHigh-visibility clothing, footwear – the last line of defence

Where a higher control was judged not practicable, the reason should be recorded. On site, ask the STMS why. If the reason no longer holds – traffic is lower than planned, or a detour is available – note it.

The hierarchy of controls ranks how effective a control is. At the top is elimination, like detouring traffic away from the work altogether. Then substitution, like portable traffic signals instead of a person at the roadside. Then isolation, such as rated barriers. Then engineering controls, such as a truck-mounted attenuator. Below those are administrative controls, like signs, speed limits and safe work methods. And at the bottom is personal protective equipment, the last line of defence. If a higher control wasn't used, the reason should be recorded. On site, ask the STMS why, and note it if that reason no longer holds.
