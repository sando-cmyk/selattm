Use the TSL, Not the “Road Works” Tick

~1 min · narrated

Police tick “road works” in only 6.7% of worksite crashes (1,038 of 15,561). The tick rate doubles at night, which fits it mostly recording road works struck as an object. Analysis that relies on that box finds 13 of the 158 deaths – it misses about 92%.

ON EVERY ASSESSMENT

When you check local crash history, or read a client safety case, make sure worksite crashes were identified by temporary speed limit – not the road works tick alone.

Source: Sela Civil Advisory, SELA-RPT-2026-006 Who Gets Hurt at Our Worksites (12 Nov 2026), from NZTA Crash Analysis System open data, Jan 2006 – Nov 2026.

Here's something every assessor should know. Police tick the road works box in only six point seven per cent of worksite crashes. If you rely on that box alone, you'll miss about ninety-two per cent of worksite deaths. The most reliable marker is the temporary speed limit recorded in the crash. So whenever you look up crash history for a site, or read a client's safety case, check that worksite crashes were identified by temporary speed limit, not just the road works tick.
