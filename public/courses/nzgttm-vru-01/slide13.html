How Cyclists Use the Road

~1 min · narrated
  • Cyclists ride as far left as practical – but may take the lane where that isn’t safe.
  • Speeds vary widely: a confident commuter at 30 km/h or more, a child at walking pace.
  • Low-powered devices such as e-scooters may use the footpath or the roadway.
WATCH FOR

Squeeze cyclists out of the cycle lane onto a road that feels unsafe and many will ride the footpath – moving the risk onto pedestrians.

KEY IDEA

Give cyclists a clear, direct line through or around the site, with warning in good time.

Module three: cyclists and micromobility. Cyclists are the fastest vulnerable road users on your site, and the least likely to follow a long detour. By law, cyclists ride as far to the left as is practical, but where that isn't safe they may take the lane, riding in the middle so vehicles can't squeeze past. Speeds vary a lot. A confident commuter may be travelling at thirty kilometres an hour or more, while a child might be at walking pace. Just because some riders are slow doesn't mean they all will be. And if your site squeezes cyclists out of the cycle lane and onto a road that feels unsafe, many will move onto the footpath. That moves the risk onto pedestrians, so plan the cycle route as carefully as the footpath.
