Three principles run through everything we do for vulnerable road users. They match the principles used for permanent pedestrian design, so temporary and permanent works follow the same approach. First, safe. Do a proper risk assessment that covers the environment, the activity and any new risks your controls create. Always look for the lowest total risk. Moving people away from the work is good, unless it means they now have to cross a busy road. And make safety the priority, ahead of cost, delay or convenience. Second, obvious. The layout should be clear at a glance, for everyone, in any light and any weather. Third, smooth and stable. Keep routes step-free, firm and clear of obstacles, and keep them as short and straight as possible. Long, winding routes tire people out and tempt them to break the rules.
