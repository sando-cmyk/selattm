Sharing the Lane with Vehicles

~1 min · narrated

Lane width decides how cyclists and vehicles share safely.

Lane widthWhat happens
4.25 m or moreRoom for vehicles to pass a cyclist safely
Between 3.25 m and 4.25 mDanger zone – drivers squeeze past. Narrow it further
3.25 m or lessCyclist takes the lane; vehicles follow behind
SUPPORT IT

Warn drivers that cyclists may be in the lane, slow the traffic, and manage parking so it doesn’t eat into the shared lane.

Sometimes cyclists have to share the traffic lane. Lane width decides how that works. If the lane is four point two five metres or wider, there's room for vehicles to pass a cyclist safely. Below that, cyclists and vehicles shouldn't ride side by side, because drivers will try to squeeze past. At three point two five metres or less, make the lane narrow enough that the cyclist rides in the middle, takes the lane, and vehicles follow behind. The in-between widths are the dangerous ones, where a driver thinks they can just fit. Use signs to tell drivers cyclists may be in the lane, slow the traffic down, and manage parking so it doesn't eat into the shared lane.
