Other Side, Detours and Covered Walkways

~1 min · narrated

“Use the other side” and detours

  • Low on the list: compliance is proven to be poor
  • Only once the better options are ruled out
  • Detours must be short, clearly signed and step-free all the way
  • A long or rough detour fails the people who need it most

Covered walkways

  • 1.8 m clear width (2.5 m in city centres)
  • 2.5 m headroom
  • Lit at all times
  • Handrails 0.85–1.0 m high if one side is open

Next: the Module 2 knowledge check – five questions, 80% to pass.

Sending pedestrians to the footpath on the other side of the road sits low on the list for a reason. Research shows people often don't do it. They walk past the signs and into the site, or cross wherever suits them. Only use it once the better options have been ruled out. Detours work only if they're short, clearly signed and step-free all the way. If the detour is long or rough, the people who most need it will be the least able to use it. Where the footpath stays open under a risk of falling objects, use a covered walkway. Give a clear width of one point eight metres, or two point five metres in a busy city centre, with two point five metres of headroom. Light it at all times, and fit handrails if one side is open.
