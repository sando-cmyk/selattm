How People Really Behave

~1 min · narrated

Your controls only work if people follow them. Plan for real behaviour, not ideal behaviour.

1Least resistance

People choose the shortest, most comfortable route. A long or rough route won’t be used.

2Shortcuts

Confident people cut through – cyclists ride the footpath, walkers cross mid-block.

3Detours are hard

Less mobile people may avoid a detour altogether and cross somewhere unsafe.

4Distraction

Phones and headphones mean signs and sounds get missed.
WHAT IT MEANS FOR TTM

Make the safe route the easiest route, keep it simple, and give the same message more than one way – fencing, signs, markings and people.

Your controls only work if people follow them, so you have to plan for how people actually behave. People take the path of least resistance and most comfort. If your route is long, bumpy or confusing, people won't use it. Confident people take shortcuts: a cyclist rides the footpath to get past the site, or a pedestrian crosses mid-block instead of walking to your temporary crossing. Less mobile people find detours hard: a wheelchair user faced with a rough, complicated detour may simply cross the road where it isn't safe. People judge risk differently, and many are distracted, on their phones or wearing headphones. So keep it simple. Make the safe route the easiest route, and repeat the same message in more than one way: fencing, signs, markings, and people where needed.
