Open, Move or Close?

~2 min (interactive) · narrated

1Keep it open

Best: intuitive, better for disabled people, usually cheaper.

2Move it nearby

A temporary stop when the permanent one can’t be used safely.

3Close it

Last resort – it hits people with mobility impairments hardest.

A stop can stay open only if these all stay safe: boarding and alighting, the walk to and from the stop (including crossings), and people and traffic moving past. Involve the road controlling authority (RCA) and public transport authority (PTA) early.

Suburban stop – berm trench beside it

Keep it open. Fence the trench, keep a 1.8 m path past the shelter and a clear boarding area. Buses still use the stop, so 5 working days’ notice to the RCA/PTA.

Hospital stop – kerb rebuild in front

Move it nearby. Same route, within 100 m or in clear sight, with step-free boarding and a safe crossing – vital for patients and visitors. Moved with no bus diversion: 10 working days’ notice.

Stop inside a full road closure

Close it (buses detour). Agree alternatives with the RCA/PTA. Closure with a diversion: 6 weeks’ notice. Post a notice and map at the stop 2 working days before.

Module four: bus stops affected by TTM. When your site affects a bus stop, there are three options, in this order. Keep the stop open where it is, move it to a temporary stop nearby, or, as a last resort, close it. Keeping it open is best. It's more intuitive for passengers, better for disabled people and usually cheaper. But a stop can only stay open if three things stay safe: getting on and off the bus, the walk to and from the stop including any road crossings, and people and traffic moving past it. Talk to the road controlling authority and the public transport authority early. Is the stop a terminus, a transfer point or a school stop? How busy is it? Does it have a shelter? A stop that looks minor to you may matter a lot to the people who use it. Try the activity: three stops, three decisions.
