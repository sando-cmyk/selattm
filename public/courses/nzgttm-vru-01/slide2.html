Why This Matters

~1 min · narrated

In October 2019 a cyclist was killed at a construction site in Christchurch. The enforceable undertakings that followed produced an industry practice note on protecting vulnerable road users (VRUs) in TTM, published by Civil Contractors NZ in December 2023.

How we have usually planned

  • Vehicles first
  • People walking and cycling fitted in last
  • VRUs get the least attention, despite the highest risk

What good practice asks

  • Design for VRUs first
  • Then fit the work and the vehicles around them
  • Understand why – controls without the why don’t work
KEY IDEA

Road workers walk and cycle too. A site that is safer for the public is safer for their families as well.

Module one: who vulnerable road users are. In October twenty nineteen, a cyclist was killed at a construction site in Christchurch. The enforceable undertakings that followed led to an industry practice note on protecting vulnerable road users in temporary traffic management, published by Civil Contractors New Zealand in December twenty twenty-three. This course draws on that practice note. Its central message is simple. People walking and cycling are the most at risk on our sites, yet they usually get the least attention in planning. We have a habit of designing for vehicles first and fitting people on foot and on bikes in afterwards. The practice note asks us to do the opposite: design for vulnerable road users first. And remember, the people who work on our sites walk and cycle too, and so do their families.
