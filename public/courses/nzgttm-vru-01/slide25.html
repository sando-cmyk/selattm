Changes, Records and Reporting

~1 min · narrated

Changes

  • If you see something that should be safer, you must try to fix it
  • Every change must leave the site safer than before
  • Minor changes: within the TMP, noted on it
  • Major changes: stop work, make safe, back to the TTM planner

Records

  • Changes to the TMP, near misses and incidents
  • What, when, where, who, and how it was fixed
  • Photos, or a short narrated video of your site walk
  • Agreed critical notifications: injury, vehicle breach, equipment failure
ASK “WHAT IF?”

What if a blind person walked through here? What if this fence blew over? Plan the answer before it happens.

Things change on site, and most changes are small ones that make the site safer. If you're in charge and you see something that should be safer, you must try to make it safer, and any change must leave the site safer than it was. Minor changes, like moving a cone or a sign, can be made within the TMP and noted on it. Major changes mean stopping work, making the site safe, and going back to the TTM planner. Think ahead: what if a blind person walked through here? What if this fence blew over? Record what happens: changes to the TMP, near misses and incidents, what went wrong, when, who was involved, and how it was fixed. Photos help, and a short narrated video of your site walk is often better than a written report. Agree a list of critical notifications with your company, such as any injury, a vehicle breaching the site or equipment failure. That's the end of the content.
