THE SITE

A painted cycle lane on a 50 km/h arterial is closed for 40 m for a kerb repair. Tap the three points where a rider would be confused or squeezed.

0 of 3 explored

Sign at the cones

The only warning sign is at the start of the closure. A rider at 30 km/h has no time to look back and merge. Fix: put the warning about 25 m before the hazard, visible from at least 32 m.

Sharp shift

Cones swing riders 1.5 m sideways over 3 m – a 1:2 taper. Fix: lengthen it to 1:8 because riders are merging with traffic (12 m for a 1.5 m shift), with cones at 1 m.

3.8 m shared lane

Too narrow to pass a cyclist safely, but wide enough that drivers try. Fix: narrow it to 3.25 m or less so riders take the lane, or keep 4.25 m; add “cyclists in lane” signs and slow the traffic.

Because cyclists travel fast, they need warning in good time. Signs should be visible to cyclists from twenty metres away at thirty kilometres an hour, rising to sixty metres at seventy. Warning signs should sit roughly half the speed in metres before the hazard: fifteen metres at thirty, thirty-five metres at seventy. When you shift cyclists sideways, use a taper no steeper than one in six, or one in eight if they're merging with traffic. Cones beside a cycle path can be five metres apart, two point five at bends to slow riders, and one metre through tapers. Now ride the site. A cycle lane is closed for a kerb repair.
