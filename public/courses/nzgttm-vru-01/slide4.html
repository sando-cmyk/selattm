Design for Disabled People First

~1 min · narrated

People are disabled by environments that don’t work for them – and a TTM site can easily become one. Disabilities can also be temporary, like a broken leg.

TypeWhat it can mean on your site
PhysicalTrouble with narrow or angled routes, rough surfaces, slopes, level changes and long detours
CognitiveNeeding more time to react; confusing signs; flashing lights or noise causing anxiety
SensoryCan’t see signs, can’t hear a reversing beeper, can’t tell colours apart

These often overlap – an older person may have low vision and poor balance.

THE RULE FOR THIS COURSE

If it’s safe for disabled people, it’s safe for everybody.

You'll notice this course puts a strong focus on disabled people, and that's deliberate. People are disabled by environments that don't work for them, and a TTM site can quickly become one of those environments. Think about three kinds of difference. Physical: difficulty with narrow or angled routes, rough surfaces, slopes, steps and long detours. Cognitive: some people need more time to react, some find signs confusing, and flashing lights or loud noise can make people anxious. And sensory: people who are blind or have low vision, people who are Deaf or hard of hearing, and people with colour blindness. These often overlap, especially with age. For these people, even a small shortfall, like one missing fence panel or a sign that sticks out a little too far, can be the difference between getting through safely or not. The good news is the rule that runs through this whole course: if it's safe for disabled people, it's safe for everybody.
