Who Counts as a Vulnerable Road User?

~2 min (interactive) · narrated

Anyone on or near your site who is not in a motor vehicle and not part of the crew. They travel at different speeds, don’t keep to lanes, and have no cab, horn or crumple zone.

On foot

  • Children and older people
  • People with prams
  • People with walking frames, crutches or canes

On wheels

  • Cyclists, e-scooters, skateboards
  • Wheelchair users
  • Mobility scooters

0 of 6 explored

Person with a cane

Can’t read your signs. Follows the kerb, tactile paving and continuous edges. A gap in fencing, a sign leg in the path or tactile paving left in place at a closed crossing can lead them straight into the work.

Wheelchair user

Needs width, a firm level surface and ramps – no steps, lips or soft ground. A long or rough detour may make them give up and cross the road somewhere unsafe.

Parent with a pram

Needs the same width and step-free surface as a wheelchair. Often in a hurry, and may push the pram onto the road if the path narrows or ends.

Child walking to school

Small, hard for drivers to see, and slower to judge traffic speed and gaps. Likely to take the shortest route, even if it isn’t the one you signed.

Commuter cyclist

The fastest VRU on site. Needs warning in good time and a clear line. If squeezed out of the cycle lane, may ride the footpath and put pedestrians at risk.

Jogger with headphones

Won’t hear reversing alarms or a spotter’s call. Needs physical guidance – fencing, cone bars, a clear path – not just sound or a sign.

So who counts as a vulnerable road user? In simple terms, anyone on or near your site who isn't in a motor vehicle and isn't part of your crew. That includes people on foot: children, older people, people pushing prams, people using walking frames or canes. And people on wheels: cyclists, e-scooter riders, skateboarders, people in wheelchairs and on mobility scooters. They move at different speeds from traffic, they don't stay in lanes, and they have no cab, horn or crumple zone to protect them. Two groups aren't covered here. Motorcyclists are vulnerable, but they travel in traffic lanes at traffic speeds, so they need different measures. And workers are covered by your own site safety systems. Now try the activity.
