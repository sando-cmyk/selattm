Activity: Spot the Trip

~2 min (interactive) · narrated
ItemGuidance
Kerb rampsAt least 1.2 m wide; 1:12 grade (1:8 only if monitored with very non-slip surfacing); 1.2 × 1.2 m landing top and bottom where space is tight
Trip hazardsAnything thicker than a pencil – hoses, cables, sign feet, surface lips
Exclusion zone0.6 m between people and a hazard; don’t place fencing hard against the hazard
THE SITE

A footpath diversion past a trench outside a row of shops. Tap the five problems a wheelchair user with low vision would meet.

0 of 5 explored

Pinch point

The path narrows to under 1.2 m beside a stack of pipes. A wheelchair or pram can’t get through. Fix: move the stockpile and keep at least 1.2 m (preferably 1.8 m).

Cable across the path

A power lead runs across the walkway. It’s thicker than a pencil and catches canes and small wheels. Fix: route it overhead or under a proper flat cable cover.

Sign in the path

A sign and its feet stand in the walking line. A blind person’s cane may miss the leg and they walk into the panel. Fix: place signs clear of the path; space signs further apart if needed.

No kerb ramp

The diversion drops off the kerb into the road with no ramp. Fix: a ramp at least 1.2 m wide at 1:12, keyed in if it’s asphalt, with level landings.

Gap in the fencing

One panel is missing next to the trench. People following the fence line by touch walk straight through it. Fix: continuous fencing, set back from the edge with a 0.6 m exclusion zone.

Smooth and stable is about the details. Kerb ramps should be at least one point two metres wide, with a gradient of one in twelve. One in eight is only acceptable if the ramp is monitored and has very good non-slip surfacing. Where the ramp meets a narrow space, give a level landing of one point two by one point two metres at the top and bottom so a wheelchair can turn. Here's a simple test for trip hazards: anything thicker than a pencil, whether it's a hose, a cable, a sign foot or a lip in the surface, is a trip hazard. Where people pass close to a hazard, leave a point six metre exclusion zone. And don't put fencing hard up against a hazard, because fencing deflects when it's hit. Now try the activity: a footpath diversion with five problems. Tap each one.
