Keep It Open, or Separate by Time

~1 min · narrated

1. Keep existing facilities

  • Plan the work method around the footpath, cycle lane or crossing
  • Check a tight work area won’t make the job longer or less safe for the crew
  • Fence between the work and the public
  • Check the existing path is in good condition

2. Separation by time

  • Pause the work, or hold people in a safe waiting area, while they pass
  • Plan stoppages around busy walking and cycling times
  • Stop/go can include a phase just for cyclists
  • Someone must see people coming in time to stop work
KEEP HOLDS SHORT

People – especially cyclists – lose patience. Long holds lead to people pushing through. A sign alone won’t manage it: blind people can’t read it.

The best option is to keep the existing footpath, cycle lane or crossing working. Plan the work method around it. Check whether squeezing the work into a tight space would make the job take longer, or make it less safe for the crew. You'll still usually need fencing between the work and the public. If you can't keep it open all the time, separate people by time. Pause the work, or hold people in a safe waiting area, while they pass through. Stop/go can even have a phase just for cyclists. Look at when people walk and cycle past, and plan stoppages around the busy times. Keep holds short. People, especially cyclists, lose patience, and long holds lead to people pushing through. Make sure someone can see people coming in time to stop the work.
