BEST AVOIDED

Temporary crossings over roads wider than 10 m aren’t advised. Look for another solution first; if you must, add a refuge so people cross in two stages.

Sometimes you need to move people across the road on a temporary crossing. Make it one point eight metres wide so wheelchairs and pedestrians can pass. Drivers approaching from every direction need enough stopping sight distance to see people crossing and stop in time. That's forty metres at thirty kilometres an hour, seventy-five metres at fifty, and over a hundred metres at seventy. Try to get speeds down to seventy or less near people walking and cycling. A crossing longer than ten metres should have a refuge so people can cross in two stages. But the practice note is clear: temporary crossings over roads wider than ten metres are best avoided. Look for another solution first. And remember lowest total risk. A crossing moves people away from your work, but it also puts them in front of traffic.
