Setting Up a Temporary Stop

~1 min · narrated
CheckGuidance
LocationSame route; within 100 m or in clear sight of the original; not within about 150 m of the next stop
Getting thereA safe crossing nearby matters more than the distance rules
The busRoom to pull in and out; clearance for tail swing, signs, trees and street furniture (and double-decker height); no multiple lane changes to reach it

If you're moving the stop, the temporary stop should be on the same route, within a hundred metres of the original or in clear sight of it, and not within about a hundred and fifty metres of the next stop along. But a safe crossing point near the temporary stop matters more than those distances. People shouldn't have to cross a busy road with no safe crossing to reach it. The bus needs room to pull in and out, and clearance for tail swing, signs, trees and street furniture, including height for double-deckers. On a multi-lane road, buses shouldn't have to make several lane changes to reach the stop. The stop can't sit in a no-stopping zone unless that's changed legally, can't block sight lines from side roads, and passengers shouldn't board across a driveway. Boarding must be step-free, even if that means a temporary ramp. Consider lighting, seating and shelter for longer jobs.
