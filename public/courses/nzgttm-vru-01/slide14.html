Temporary Separated Cycleways

~1 min · narrated

Keep cyclists apart from vehicles. Keep the permanent width if you can; if not:

SituationMinimum width
Permanent speed 50 km/h or less1.0 m
Permanent speed over 50 km/h1.5 m

Clearance beside fences or barriers+0.5 m
THE DIVERSION POINT

Riders will try to cut through the cones where the route changes. Use something solid there to show them where to go.

Where the work takes over a cycle lane, the next best option is a temporary separated cycleway: a route that keeps cyclists apart from vehicles. As always, keep the permanent width if you can. If you can't, use at least one metre where the permanent speed limit is fifty kilometres an hour or less, one point five metres above fifty, and two metres if it's a two-way cycleway. Leave half a metre of clearance beside fences, barriers or other obstacles, so riders can use the full width without clipping something. And make sure cyclists can't cut through the cones at the diversion point. That is exactly where riders will try to shortcut, so use something solid there to show them where to go.
