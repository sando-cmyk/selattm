TMP DOESN’T FIT?

Tell the TTM planner straight away. Minor changes are noted on the TMP; major changes mean stop work and minimise the site until it’s fixed.

Any TTM that affects a bus stop must be approved in advance by the road controlling authority or public transport authority. The recommended lead times are five working days if the stop stays open and buses aren't affected, ten working days if buses may be delayed, or if the stop is moved without a diversion, and six weeks if the stop is closed or buses are diverted. Include the stop details, the dates, the effect on pedestrians, photos of the stop, and your risk register. On site, the STMS covers or removes the old bus stop sign, and puts up a notice with a map showing the temporary stop two working days before the change. The temporary stop is set up before the old one closes, with TEMPORARY BUS STOP and TEMPORARY BUS STOP AHEAD signs, cones at two and a half metre spacing along thirty-three metres, and no-parking signs at each end. If the TMP doesn't suit the site, tell the TTM planner straight away. Next is the Module four knowledge check.
