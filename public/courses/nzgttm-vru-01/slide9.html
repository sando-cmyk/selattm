Passing baysAt least 1.5 m long, widening to 1.8 m, no more than 60 m apart

When you narrow a footpath or build a temporary one, keep the permanent width if you can. If you can't, the preferred width for any temporary pedestrian route is one point eight metres. That lets two wheelchairs pass. You can go down to one point two metres, but only for the shortest possible length. If a stretch narrower than one point eight metres runs for more than sixty metres, add passing bays. Each bay should be at least one point five metres long and widen the path to one point eight metres, and they should be no more than sixty metres apart. Keep the crossfall no steeper than one in fifty, enough to drain water but not enough to tip a wheelchair. When you divert people off the footpath, the order is: first the property side of the path, then the berm, and only then a separated space at the edge of the road, moving any cycle lane over too.
