3. Checking the Risk Assessment Onsite: The G-Checks

~6 min read

Learning Outcome 1a asks you to check that the risk assessment includes any new risks and still aligns with the TMP. Waka Kotahi's own quality assurance system gives a structured way to do exactly that — a set of checks known as the G-checks, used in a Site Condition Review (SCR).

Two of the G-checks map directly onto your role:

G4 — Is the OSR completed, and a fair reflection of the actual site condition? In other words: does the paperwork still match what you can see in front of you?

G7 — Assess the TMP relative to the actual environment, workspace, activity, and hazards. In other words: has anything about the site itself changed since the TMP was approved?

You don't need to run a formal SCR yourself to use this thinking. Every time you arrive on site, or notice something's different partway through a shift, ask those same two questions: does the risk assessment still reflect what's actually here, and does the TMP still fit the environment? If either answer is no, that's a new risk that needs raising — with your supervisor, following your organisation's requirements, before you carry on.

By the end of this section you can:

  • State what G4 and G7 check, and why they matter to your day-to-day role
  • Apply the “does the paperwork match the site” question to a live TTM environment
KEY IDEA

Key idea: a risk assessment that was accurate at 7am can be wrong by 9am. Weather changes, traffic volumes shift, a detour opens up unexpectedly. Checking alignment isn't a one-off box-tick — it's something you keep doing.
