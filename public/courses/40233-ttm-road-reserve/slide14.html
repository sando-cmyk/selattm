4. Spot the Change: When Site Conditions Move Away From the TMP

~10 min read (interactive)

Two example scenarios, worked through here so the content exists outside the interactive build as well:

Scenario 1 — Sudden heavy rain. The TMP was approved for dry conditions. Heavy rain starts, pooling water appears near the work zone, and visibility of your signage and TTM devices drops. Inclement weather is a recognised risk category — it changes both the road surface condition and the visibility of your traffic control devices. The right response: pause and reassess, record the new condition against the risk assessment, and raise it with your supervisor — the TMP may need additional controls (extra warning distance, reduced TSL, additional lighting) before work continues.

Scenario 2 — Unexpected local congestion. A nearby event or a crash on an alternative route pushes far more traffic through your site than the TMP anticipated. Traffic queues and delays are a recognised risk category too — and end-of-queue collisions are a specific, serious consequence of queues the TMP didn't plan for. The right response: the same pattern — record it, raise it, and don't assume the original TMP's queue-management assumptions still hold.

In both cases, the pattern is the same: notice the change, check it against the TMP and risk assessment, and escalate rather than deciding alone that it's probably fine.

CAUTION
