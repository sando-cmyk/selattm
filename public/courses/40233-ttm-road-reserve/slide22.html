4. Actively Controlling Vehicle Movements

~6 min read

Learning Outcome 1d requires that vehicle movements and vulnerable road users within the road reserve are actively controlled — actively being the operative word. A TTM layout that's correctly installed still needs someone actively managing what happens within it.

Site entry and exit is the highest-risk vehicle movement you'll manage. Before any vehicle enters or leaves the site: tell drivers about the TTM setup before they arrive — where to enter, park, and where to stay away from; control exactly when entry and exit happens, using a spotter or another TTM worker where needed; and make sure it never happens without your knowledge.

Around live traffic more generally, active control means positioning yourself where drivers can see you and where you have a clear escape route, using clear and consistent hand signals or a sign, and never assuming a driver has seen you just because you can see them.

Reversing and plant movements need a dedicated spotter whenever sightlines are limited — this is an administrative control from the hierarchy of controls you learned about in Module 2, and it only works if it's actually followed, every time, not just when it's convenient.

By the end of this section you can:

  • Describe the specific risks of site entry and exit, and how to control them
  • Explain what “active” control of vehicle movements means in practice, beyond correct signage
CAUTION

Site entry and exit has killed people. Make it a deliberate, communicated, spotter-supported process every single time — not a quick check over your shoulder.
