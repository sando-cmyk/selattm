2. The TTM Zone — Where the Job Actually Happens

~4 min read

Inside the road reserve, your day-to-day work happens inside a more specific space: the TTM zone. Standard 40233 defines the TTM zone as ‘the section of road defined at each end by advance warning and end of works signs or between vehicles in a mobile operation, including the vehicles themselves.’

Two things follow directly from that definition. First, the TTM zone has a defined start and a defined end — it is marked by signage (or, in a mobile operation, by the working vehicles themselves), not by wherever work happens to be occurring at a given moment. Second, a mobile operation's TTM zone moves with the vehicles — the zone is the vehicles, not a fixed patch of road.

Everything covered in the rest of this module — the hazards, how they cause harm, and how you're expected to actively control vehicle movements and protect vulnerable road users — happens inside this zone, and inside the wider road reserve it sits within.

A TTM zone runs start of works to end of works: advance warning → transition/taper → work area → termination.

By the end of this section you can:

  • State the standard's definition of the TTM zone
  • Explain the difference between a fixed TTM zone (defined by signage) and a mobile one (defined by the working vehicles)
NOTE

Organisational requirements — the policies, TMPs, practice notes, and procedures your employer documents — apply throughout the TTM zone and the road reserve. Later modules in this course cover following those requirements in detail; this module focuses on recognising hazards, which is the foundation everything else builds on.
