6. Dealing With Changes, Incidents & Emergencies Onsite

~5 min read

Most changes you make onsite won't be emergencies — they'll be small adjustments that make things safer, and you're expected to make them. If you see something that should be safer and you're in a position to fix it, you should try to.

Anticipate ‘what if.’ Don't wait for a problem to figure out what to do. Regularly walk through scenarios with your team: what if this barrier fails, what if traffic suddenly increases. Thinking ahead prevents panic and produces a faster, safer response when something does go wrong.

Be ready for the unexpected — your radio within reach, vehicles parked where they're safe but accessible, your crew briefed on emergency procedures.

When something does go wrong, report it, whether it's a close call or an actual incident: what happened, when and where, who was involved or saw it, what (if anything) was damaged, and how it was fixed. Anything on your organisation's critical notifications list goes to your supervisor immediately, not at the end of the day.

Dealing with changes, incidents & emergencies: notice → check → escalate.

By the end of this section you can:

  • Explain the difference between a routine safety improvement and an incident requiring urgent reporting
  • Describe what information a report should capture
NOTE

Any change you make onsite must result in it being safer than it was before — that's the test for whether a change is yours to make, or a decision that needs to go to your supervisor first.
