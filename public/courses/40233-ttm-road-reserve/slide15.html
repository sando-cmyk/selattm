5. Organisational Requirements: Policies, Procedures & Practice Notes

~5 min read

Standard 40233 expects you to follow organisational requirements — and understanding where those requirements come from helps you take them seriously rather than treating them as red tape.

One example is the operational practice note — a document some organisations create for activities they do repeatedly, where the risks and controls are consistent site to site (for example, a standard approach to line marking, or to a routine bus-stop closure). A well-built practice note documents the activity, its risks, the chosen controls, the residual risk that's left over, and a diagram showing the TTM zone and exclusion zone. It exists so the same, tested thinking gets applied every time — not reinvented, and not skipped, on a busy day.

More broadly, your organisation's policies, procedures, and health and safety plans exist for the same reason: to apply the law and good practice consistently. Following them isn't just about compliance — it's the mechanism by which lessons learned on one site get carried to the next.

Where organisational requirements come from — each level narrows down to the plan you actually follow on site.

By the end of this section you can:

  • Explain the purpose of an operational practice note
  • Explain why following organisational requirements matters beyond compliance
NOTE

You won't usually write a practice note yourself at this level — but recognising when one applies, and following it, is exactly the kind of organisational requirement Standard 40233 expects you to meet.
