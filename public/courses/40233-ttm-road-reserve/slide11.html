1. What a Risk Assessment & a TMP Actually Are

~6 min read

Two documents sit behind every TTM site you assist on: the risk assessment and the Traffic Management Plan.

The risk assessment — often called the on-site risk assessment, or OSR — identifies the hazards specific to that site and activity, and records the controls chosen to manage them. It's the working record of ‘what could hurt someone here, and what are we doing about it.’

The Traffic Management Plan, or TMP, is the approved plan for how traffic, work, and safety are managed at the site — the layout, the signage, the temporary speed limit, the staffing. It's approved by the Road Controlling Authority (RCA) before work starts, and it's what your organisation and the RCA agreed would keep the site safe.

Standard 40233 also uses the term organisational requirements — the policies, procedures, methodologies, health and safety plans, TMPs, practice notes, contract work programmes, and quality plans your employer documents. These sit above any single site: they're how your organisation applies the law and good practice consistently, site after site.

KEY IDEA

Key idea: the TMP is the plan; the risk assessment is the reasoning behind it. When a site changes, it's the risk assessment that tells you whether the TMP still holds up — or whether something now needs to change.
