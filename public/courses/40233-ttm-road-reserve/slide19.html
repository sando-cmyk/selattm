1. Before You Start: Driving, Parking & Positioning Safely

~6 min read

Safe practice starts before a single cone is placed. Driving into a site to set up carries its own risk, especially where vulnerable road users are present.

Blind spots — know where yours are. A cyclist, in particular, can close the distance into a blind spot faster than you expect. Check constantly, not just once.

Safe speed — drive slowly, especially near crossings, cycle lanes, and busy footpaths, so you have time to spot and react to someone who appears unexpectedly.

Parking — park so you don't block sight lines between road users; keep your distance from crossings and intersections so people can still see each other; make sure you're visible and don't move your vehicle unexpectedly; don't block access to bus stops or walkways; and check for people before you open a door.

These aren't abstract courtesies — a vehicle arriving at or leaving a worksite is one of the highest-risk moments for a vulnerable road user near TTM. Getting this right, every time, before you even start setting up, is part of what Standard 40233 means by safe practice.

By the end of this section you can:

  • List the key safe-driving and parking practices for arriving at and setting up a TTM site
  • Explain why site arrival is a high-risk moment for vulnerable road users specifically
CAUTION

People have been killed by vehicles entering and exiting worksites. Treat every site entry and exit as a moment that needs your full attention, not a routine you can do on autopilot.
