Where These Requirements Come From

~4 min read

The content in this module traces back to specific sources, same as everywhere else in this course.

1. Skill Standard 40233 (NZQA / Waihanga Ara Rau), version 1, registered 29 August 2024 — sets the learning outcome and assessment criteria this module supports, defines the road reserve and TTM zone, and names the first indicative content point this module teaches directly: common onsite hazards in the TTM industry and how they cause harm.

2. WorkSafe New Zealand — Keeping healthy and safe while working on the road or roadside — the good-practice guideline named in the standard's own resources section; source for the safety-risk hazard categories used in the Hazard Spotting activity.

3. New Zealand Guide to Temporary Traffic Management (NZGTTM), 2023, and its Identification of TTM risks supplement — source for the specific issue/risk/impact examples (traffic queues, vulnerable road users, plant access, environmental conditions) used throughout this module.

4. Practice Note: Protecting Vulnerable Road Users in TTM Environments (Civil Contractors NZ, 2024) — source for the vulnerable-road-user hazard and the real-incident case study.

5. Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 (HSWA) — the underlying legal duty behind every hazard and control covered here: a PCBU's duty to eliminate, or so far as reasonably practicable minimise, risk.

Where these requirements come from.

The content in this module traces back to five sources. The primary source is Skill Standard four-oh-two-three-three itself, version 1, registered the twenty-ninth of August, twenty twenty-four — it sets the learning outcome and assessment criteria, defines the road reserve and T-T-M zone, and names the first indicative content point this module teaches directly.

The second source is WorkSafe New Zealand's guide, Keeping healthy and safe while working on the road or roadside — the good-practice guideline named in the standard's own resources section.

The third is the New Zealand Guide to Temporary Traffic Management, N-Z-G-T-T-M, twenty twenty-three, and its Identification of T-T-M risks supplement — source for the specific issue and risk examples used throughout this module.

The fourth is the Practice Note, Protecting Vulnerable Road Users in T-T-M Environments, from Civil Contractors New Zealand, twenty twenty-four — source for the vulnerable-road-user hazard and the real-incident case study.

And the fifth is the Health and Safety at Work Act twenty fifteen, H-S-W-A — the underlying legal duty behind every hazard and control covered here.
