1. The Road Reserve — Legal Boundaries & the Six-Metre Airspace

~6 min read

Everything in this course happens inside a legally defined space: the road reserve. Standard 40233 defines it precisely — ‘the area of land between the legal boundaries, usually fence line to fence line and including any safety run-off areas, which is dedicated to allowing the passage of road users.’ Critically, the road reserve is not just the sealed carriageway you can see. It typically extends past the kerb or edge line to the legal boundary — often a fence line — and includes any safety run-off area between the road and that boundary.

The road reserve also has a vertical dimension: it ‘includes an air space of six metres directly above the road surface.’ That six-metre airspace matters operationally, not just legally — any plant, structure, load, or overhead service that intrudes into it is inside the same regulated space as the traffic lane itself, and any activity that affects that airspace (a crane, a raised tipper body, an excavator boom, overhead lines) needs to be accounted for in the same way a lane closure would be.

Legal boundary / fence line

Definition: “The area of land between the legal boundaries, usually fence line to fence line…” This is the outer edge of the road reserve.

Why it matters: Anything inside it, even off the sealed road, is still inside the regulated space.

Safety run-off area

Definition: “…including any safety run-off areas…”

Why it matters: The space between the road and the legal boundary, dedicated to allowing the safe passage of road users, not just spare land.

Sealed carriageway

Definition: The trafficked surface itself.

Why it matters: Only one part of the full road reserve — not the whole of it, even though it's what most people picture when they hear “the road”.

Six-metre air space

Definition: “…the road reserve also includes an air space of six metres directly above the road surface.”

Why it matters: Plant, loads, and overhead services inside this height band are inside the same regulated space as the traffic lane — this comes back directly in the Hazard Spotting activity.

KEY IDEA

Key idea: the road reserve is bigger than the road. A hazard doesn't have to touch the sealed carriageway to be inside the road reserve — a fence-line encroachment, a run-off area obstruction, or an overhead intrusion into the six-metre airspace all count.
