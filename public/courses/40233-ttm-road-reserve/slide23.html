5. Protecting Vulnerable Road Users

~6 min read

A vulnerable road user is anyone walking, cycling, using a wheelchair or mobility scooter, or otherwise not in a motor vehicle, who isn't part of your TTM crew. They're hurt more easily than anyone else on or near a TTM site, and Standard 40233 makes protecting them an explicit assessment criterion.

Practical steps: make sure pedestrian and cyclist routes past your site are safe, obvious, smooth, and stable — not just technically present. Watch how people actually move through your site; hesitation or a close call is a sign the layout isn't working as intended, whatever the plan said. If you make a site safe for people with disabilities, you generally make it safe for everybody else too.

If you think someone might need help — for example, a person who is blind or has low vision approaching your site — ask them directly: ‘Would you like some help?’ Let them tell you what, if anything, they need. If someone asks to be guided, offer your arm to hold as you walk them through.

Keep watching for change. New risks to vulnerable road users often come from something that's changed on site since the plan was made — which connects straight back to Module 2's notice → check → escalate pattern.

By the end of this section you can:

  • Define “vulnerable road user” and explain why they carry the greatest risk on a TTM site
  • Describe how to check whether your site is actually safe for vulnerable road users, and how to offer appropriate help
KEY IDEA

Key idea: don't guess what someone needs — ask. ‘Would you like some help?’ respects the person's own judgement about what kind of assistance, if any, actually helps them.
